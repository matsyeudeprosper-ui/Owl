# E013 — Can Owl's favourable regime be identified prospectively? (2026-09-12)

Light study on consumed data (V2 Jan–Jun 2026 + R0 + V1), run beside the
E011 pipeline. V3/V4 were not read for this study. Timing note required
by Part 7: the E011 V3 era result was produced at 10:57 UTC, before the
E013 gate was frozen (the gate rule was fixed in code before the run but
the run itself finished after V3 existed), so V3/V4 cannot be blind
validation for E013. Script `e013.py`, tables `results_e013.txt`,
per-trade features `trades_features_e013.csv`.

Setup: archive bid M1, one continuous series 2026-01-01 → 09-11
(365 621 bars); the structure engine run once over it (1 283 flips,
4 249 BOS signals); trade outcomes from the tick-execution lists (V2
1 136, R0 524, V1 47 = 1 707 trades). Discovery = trades before
2026-08-01 (1 374), internal check = 2026-08-01 → R0 end (286), V1
descriptive (47). All features past-only: bars up to the completed
signal bar; own-outcome features use trades entered ≥ 120 min earlier
(median holding time ≈ 40 min). Fixed windows 60 / 360 / 1440 bars.

The good period is a cheaper (spread 7 vs 12.6) and quieter (range −23%,
sd −17%) market, not a more directional one: efficiency ratios, sign
autocorrelation and market-level breakout follow-through are identical
or slightly worse. The cost-to-movement ratio improved by 13–20%, but
E011's $7 diagnostic (Jan–May still −$530 at today's spread) shows cost
alone does not explain the bad period. The one family that separates
the periods cleanly is Owl's own recent record, which is circular by
construction: a regime where the strategy has been winning is a regime
where it has been winning.

## 4–5. The one frozen gate

Rule fixed in code before the run: the discovery-monotone variable with
the largest gap and an economic direction, ON above its discovery upper
tertile boundary. Selected: **ON when Owl's last-20 TP-first rate ≥ 0.60**.

| | always-on | gated (ON) | skipped (OFF) | trades allowed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| discovery (Jan → Jul) | −$501, PF 0.87, maxDD 779 | −$27, PF 0.98, maxDD 146 | −$474, PF 0.83 | 35% |
| check (Aug → Sep 8) | +$132, PF 1.27, maxDD 83 | +$125, PF 1.40, maxDD 64 | +$7, PF 1.04 | 56% |
| V1 | −$26, PF 0.77 | −$10, PF 0.67, exp R −0.17 | −$16, PF 0.81, exp R −0.05 | 32% |

Monthly (gated / always-on): Jan −32 / −187, Feb −73 / −283, Mar +25 /
−60, Apr −13 / −103, May 0 / −111, **Jun +4 / +139**, Jul +62 / +105, Aug
+46 / +37, Sep +70 / +70. State changes: 98 in 8 months (flickers every
2–3 days); longest ON run 06-20 → 07-06, longest OFF run 04-22 → 05-07.
It switches ON for good only on 2026-06-20, three weeks after the
turn; it misses 97% of June. In the check window it keeps 95% of the
profit with 56% of the trades and cuts the drawdown by a quarter, but
the skipped trades were not worse per trade (+0.057 R vs +0.048 R
kept): it filters volume, not quality. On V1 its ON trades were worse
than its OFF trades. False-off cost over the whole period: $2 318 of
winners skipped in discovery, $182 in the check window.

This is the E010 rolling-20 detector in another guise, with the same
verdict: it cannot tell luck from regime, and its lag is about the
length of the regimes it is trying to catch.

Started in January, both versions hit the −$60 line within six weeks.
Started in June, always-on harvests $300 by September; the gate, being

harvestable system; it delays participation in the only good stretch.

## Answers

1. Objectively, June–R0 was cheaper ($7 vs $12.60) and about 20% quieter;
   it was not more directional and breakouts did not follow through more.
2. Profitability associates with Owl's own recent record (circular) and
   weakly with lower volatility in discovery, which reverses in the
   check window; not with efficiency, cadence or market follow-through.
3. Only the own-record variable identifies the favourable regime from
   past data, and only after it has begun.
4. It switches ON on 2026-06-20, three weeks after the turn, having
   flickered 40+ times before.
5. It would have avoided $474 of the $501 discovery loss, but that is the
   sample it was selected on; the January–May start still ruins a $500
   account by February.
6. It preserves 95% of the check-window profit at 56% of the trades.
7. The relationship does not survive the check window as a per-trade
   effect (skipped trades were as good as kept ones) and reverses on V1.
8. Return/DD improves in the check window (PF 1.27 → 1.40, DD 83 → 64)
   because fewer trades are taken, not because worse trades are avoided.
9. Under the withdrawal model the gated system extracts less cash ($100
   vs $300 from June) and dies later but still dies from January.
10. No regime rule worth forward observation beyond what the observer
    already records (the rolling-20 state).

**Verdict: B.** The regime difference is real and describable (cost and
volatility), but nothing available before the trades detects it in time;
the only prospective detector is Owl's own P&L, which lags by weeks and
does not improve trade quality out of the discovery window.
Production unchanged.
