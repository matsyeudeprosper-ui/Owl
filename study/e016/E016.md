## Answers

1. **Sub-minute observation?** Yes: the OKX websocket streams individual fills with exchange timestamps. The REST poll cannot.
2. **Live data source?** OKX v5 websocket `liquidation-orders` (SWAP), BTC-USDT-SWAP filter. Keep the REST recorder as the archive.
3. **Realistic end-to-end latency?** Unmeasured for BTC (no BTC fill during the 900 s probe). The shadow observer now logs exchange time vs receive time for every fill.
4. **Which window finds the E015 cascade soonest?** None cleanly. 15 s fires most often but is worst; 60 s is the least bad and catches 41 events vs E015's 113 episodes.
5. **At what delay does the edge disappear?** Under real-time detection it is not there to disappear: at zero delay the best cell is 97th percentile, at 20 s nothing beats random. The positive cells (forced-selling side, 300 s) degrade from +91 at 0 s to +57 at 20 s and +41 at 30 s, but they are post-hoc cells with n 23.
6. **Forced-selling stronger?** Yes, consistently: every forced-selling cell at 120–300 s positive, every forced-buying cell negative. Same as E015. Not part of the frozen rule, so not a result — a hypothesis for a new registration.
7. **Beats price-only controls?** Price-only large moves lose everywhere (−10), so the liquidation events are different from plain volatility; but the liquidation events themselves are not significantly positive at the frozen cells.
8. **Frequency?** About 1 event/day at W 60 s, 60% of days empty, gaps of 6 days. Economically small even if it worked.
9. **One frozen shadow rule worth forward testing?** No rule graduated. The shadow observer runs anyway, purely to measure latency and to log what the frozen (failed) rule would have done, informational only.
10. **Infrastructure for live?** Websocket client (done, `live/liq_shadow.py`), sub-second quote access (MT5 tick from the demo terminal, same feed), receive-time logging, and — if a rule ever graduates — an order path. Not built; no rule.

## Verdict: **C — the E015 zero-delay result does not reproduce under realistic event timing.**

E015's +35 points at 5 minutes was measured on 5-minute buckets with the
bucket's total known at its end. Detecting the burst fill by fill in a
15–60 s window finds fewer and smaller events, and their reversal, after
costs, is not distinguishable from random at the frozen cells, not stable
across halves, and dependent on the top two events. The one structure that
survives description is the forced-selling asymmetry (also in E015), on
23 events. If it is to be tested it must be registered as a new
experiment with the forward data (fills received after 2026-09-12 13:07
UTC) as the blind set.
