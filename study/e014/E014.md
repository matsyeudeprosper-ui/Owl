# E014 — Does a BOS event carry directional information? FOLLOW vs FADE (2026-09-12)

SCREENING ONLY: M1 first-passage with the per-bar spread, ties = stop,
no tick replay (V4 pipeline running). Event level: every BOS signal of
the frozen engine (no gate, no one-position coupling), so this measures
the signal, not the strategy. Eras: V3 2025 (8 670 events), V2 Jan–Jun
2026 (2 830), R0 (1 324), V1 (94), all consumed. V4 untouched. Script
`e014.py`, tables `results_e014.txt`.

Geometry identical on both sides: entry at the signal close (bid +
spread for the buying side), R = |close − pending-dot level|, SL 1.0R
against, TP 0.8R for; sells close on the ask (barriers shifted by the
bar spread). Random control = coin-flip direction per event with the
same times and R (200 seeds).

Both sides lose at the event level in every era. FADE beats FOLLOW in
all four eras by 0.04–0.13 R; in the two large eras FOLLOW is 2.3 sd
below random direction and FADE 2.3 sd above it. The relative polarity
is stable; the absolute edge of either side is smaller than the spread
drag (random mean −0.06 to −0.08 R).

Note the R0 line: at the event level the followed BOS lost −$379 in the
same 69 days in which the production strategy made +$237. The R0 profit
came from what the strategy does around the signal (awake gate,
one-position selection, tick fills), not from the signal's direction —
consistent with E007's finding that most of R0's edge lived in the
interaction of the entry types.

the close of a bar that closed beyond structure), not a reversal regime.

## Phase 4 — polarity through time

Monthly: FADE better in 17 of 21 months (FOLLOW better in 2025-02, -03,
-06, -10), but the difference is −0.01 to −0.15 R and the sign of each
side's expectancy is negative almost everywhere. Weekly: 90 weeks, 43
runs of the same sign against ≈ 46 expected for a random sequence, F
share 34%. Polarity does not come in persistent blocks; the FADE lean is
a constant small bias, not a regime.

## Withdrawal simulation (event-level sequences, screening)

Every version, follow / fade / polarity, is ruined within 3–22 days of
its start in V3, V2 and R0 (trading every signal, ~25 a day, at fixed
0.02: the spread bill alone is ≈ $3–4 per day). No cash is harvested
except one $100 by fade-only in V2 before ruin on 2026-01-20. Not
meaningful beyond confirming that event-level trading of either side is
not a system.

## Answers

1. BOS contains a small, stable, *negative* directional bias: the price
   is slightly more likely to retrace the breakout close than to extend
   it (fade > follow in 4/4 eras, 17/21 months, z ±2.3 on 8 670 and
   2 830 events).
2. In losing eras the opposite direction loses less; it is not
   profitable after costs (V2 fade −0.025 R, V3 −0.062 R).
3. Flip vs continuation polarity: not stable across eras.
4. Late vs early continuation: reverses between V2 and R0.
5. Polarity regimes: no persistence beyond chance (43 runs vs 46).
6. Rolling polarity score: never beats always-fade; adds noise.
7. Against random direction: FOLLOW is significantly below random,
   FADE significantly above, in the two large eras; neither is
   profitable.
8. No version survives costs at the event level in any large era.
9. No cash harvested before ruin in any version.
10. No forward-watch candidate. A tick-level confirmation is not
    warranted: there is nothing positive to confirm.

**Verdict: C** with one precise qualification — the BOS direction is
not useless as information, it is mildly *contrarian* (the breakout bar
retraces), but the effect (0.04–0.08 R) is smaller than the spread and
has no exploitable polarity structure. The strategy's R0 profit did not
come from the signal's direction. Production unchanged.
