# E009 — Fixed lot vs constant-dollar-risk sizing (2026-09-12)

Proposed by ChatGPT, executed by Claude Code. Production untouched.
Sizing is a layer over the same tick trade sequences (entries/exits never
depend on lot size). Script `e009.py` → `results_e009.txt`.

Frozen before TEST (from R0-TRAIN, gated baseline): current dollar risk
per trade (0.02 × stop) has q25 $1.96, median $2.99, q75 $5.05 (mean
$3.86, max $13.89). Models: FIX 0.02; CR(t) lot = t / stop; CA(t) lot =
t / (stop + 12 pt) (cost-aware, 12 pt = $7 spread + observed 2.5 pt
entry slip + 2.5 pt stop slip); rounding down to 0.01, min 0.01, cap
0.05. Cap rationale: the bot allows 200 pt of deviation on a fill and the
worst observed entry slip was 155 pt; at 0.05 lots a 200-pt adverse fill
costs $10, about the largest single-trade risk the account already runs.
Not a fitted number. Choices: 3 targets, 2 formulas, 1 cap.

## 1–2. Fixed lot vs constant risk (live config, 2h gate)

| model | TRAIN net / PF / maxDD | TEST net / PF / maxDD | V1 (hist.) | net/maxDD (R0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FIX 0.02 | +$102 / 1.26 / 73 | +$135 / 1.30 / 83 | −$25.6 / 53 | 237 / 83 = 2.8 |
| CR low $1.96 | +$43 / 1.19 / 38 | +$58 / 1.22 / 39 | −$13.8 / 28 | 101 / 39 = 2.6 |
| CR med $2.99 | +$52 / 1.18 / 39 | +$55 / 1.17 / 40 | −$10.8 / 31 | 107 / 40 = 2.7 |
| CR high $5.05 | +$55 / 1.13 / 47 | +$67 / 1.15 / 49 | −$22.3 / 46 | 122 / 49 = 2.5 |
| CA med $2.99 | +$57 / 1.22 / 36 | +$60 / 1.20 / 40 | −$8.9 / 30 | 117 / 40 = 2.9 |

Constant risk halves the max drawdown (83 → 40) and halves the profit
(+$237 → +$107 to +$117). Return per unit of drawdown is unchanged
(2.5–2.9 either way). TRAIN/TEST become more symmetric in dollars
(+52/+55 vs +102/+135) mostly because the magnitudes shrink; expectancy
in R is identical by construction.

By entry type (TEST): flips FIX +$40 → CR med +$6, CA +$12; continuations
FIX +$95 → CR med +$49, CA +$48. The flip book almost disappears under
constant risk because its profit sits in the wide-stop flips (E008 Q5:
+$54 in TEST), which constant risk shrinks to 0.01 lots.

## 5. Lot concentration and the narrow-stop danger

| model | lots med / p90 / max | uncapped max | trades at cap | top-10% lots P&L | narrowest stops (≤ 83 pt, n 95) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CR low | 0.01 / 0.03 / 0.05 | 0.09 | 14 | −$9 | avg lot 0.031, P&L −$21 |
| CR med | 0.01 / 0.05 / 0.05 | 0.13 | 54 | −$4 | avg lot 0.044, P&L **−$34** |
| CR high | 0.03 / 0.05 / 0.05 | 0.23 | 144 | **−$80** | avg lot 0.050, P&L **−$42** |
| CA med | 0.01 / 0.04 / 0.05 | 0.09 | 29 | −$7 | avg lot 0.039, P&L −$28 |

Yes, constant risk creates the spike: without the cap the medium target
would put 0.13 lots on the narrowest stops and the high target 0.23. Even
capped, the largest-lot tail is the losing tail in every model, and the
narrowest stops (the population E008 found to be cost-dominated) lose
$21–42 under constant risk against about −$16 at fixed lots. Constant
risk feeds lots into exactly the trades where spread eats the R. The
cost-aware formula softens this (−$34 → −$28) but does not remove it.
A hard cap is needed for any constant-risk model; 0.05 is a practical
one, and even so it binds on 54 of 524 trades at the medium target.

## 6. Cost-aware vs naive

CA med vs CR med: TRAIN +$57 vs +$52, TEST +$60 vs +$55, maxDD 36/40 vs
39/40, V1 −$8.9 vs −$10.8, narrow-stop loss −$28 vs −$34. Slightly better
everywhere, by construction (it sizes narrow stops down). Small.

## 8. Control: same lots, random assignment (1000 permutations)

| model | TRAIN structural net vs random-lot mean (beats) | maxDD structural vs random mean (lower than) | TEST net (beats) | TEST maxDD (lower than) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CR med | +$52 vs +$103 (13%) | 39 vs 78 (99.5%) | +$55 vs +$138 (7%) | 40 vs 89 (98.9%) |
| CA med | +$57 vs +$92 (19%) | 36 vs 69 (98.8%) | +$60 vs +$123 (12%) | 40 vs 81 (95.3%) |
| CR high | +$55 vs +$152 (2.5%) | 47 vs 116 (99.9%) | +$67 vs +$196 (3.6%) | 49 vs 126 (99.5%) |

This is the decisive control. Assigning the SAME lot multiset at random
earns about twice the profit of sizing by the actual stop distance, in
both halves; structural sizing only wins on drawdown. Reason: this
strategy's expectancy rises with stop distance (E008, in R as well as in
dollars), so sizing inversely to the stop systematically underweights
its best trades and overweights its cost-dominated ones. Constant risk
buys stability by cutting into the edge.

The kill line is not changed here. CORRECTED BY E010: the live kill is

max drawdown.

## Verdict: **B** — constant risk improves the risk characteristics, but the evidence is mixed; forward-watch only

1. Drawdown: halved, robustly (all statistics, both halves, bootstrap).
2. TRAIN/TEST consistency: more symmetric in dollars, unchanged in R.
3. Stale-trend dollar penalty: halved, not removed.
4. Awake gate under constant risk: still worth ~$45 in TRAIN, neutral to
   negative in TEST; about half its TRAIN dollar benefit was sizing.
5. Lot spikes: yes, real. Uncapped 0.13–0.23 lots on the narrowest stops;
   the narrow-stop population loses $21–42 under constant risk. A hard cap
   is mandatory; 0.05 binds on 10–27% of trades.
6. Cost-aware is slightly better than naive everywhere, by construction.
7. Worth forward-testing as a shadow: CA med ($2.99, +12 pt, cap 0.05) —
   positive in both halves, lowest drawdown, least narrow-stop damage.
8. Fixed 0.02 remains the better practical choice for profit: it earns
   twice as much per month, and the random-lot control shows that
   structural sizing is the WRONG direction for this strategy's edge
   (its best trades are its widest stops). What fixed 0.02 lacks is a
   kill line consistent with its own drawdown.
