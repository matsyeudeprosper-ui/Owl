# E007 — Why does the AWAKE gate work? (2026-09-12)

Proposed by ChatGPT, executed by Claude Code. Production untouched.
Discovery data: R0-TRAIN (2026-07-01 → 08-04 21:53, 259 baseline trades).
Blind validation: R0-TEST (08-04 → 09-08 07:11, 265 trades). V1 reported
as historical only. Baseline = live config (flip-BOS + continuation on
candle close, awake gate 2h, SL at pending dot, TP 0.8R, one position,
flat 0.02, tick execution, min_dist 10): TRAIN +$102.22, TEST +$134.61,
parity with the audit (+236.83 / 524) reproduced.

Scripts: `e007_lib.py` (parameterised gate), `e007_awake.py` → `results_awake.txt`
(parts 1, 2, 4, components, fixed shifts, V1), `e007_stale_stop.py` →
`results_stale_stop.txt`, `e007_placebo.py` → `results_placebo.txt` (1000 plain
circular shifts), `e007_placebo_matched.py` → `results_placebo_matched.txt`
(1000 coverage-matched shifts), `e007_randmask.py` → `results_randmask.txt`
(1000 random-eligibility masks). Distributions in `*.npy`.

A fact that shapes everything below: **the gate never touches a FLIP-BOS
entry** (the flip itself is the qualifying event at that bar). It only
decides whether CONTINUATION entries are taken. Flip-only with and without
the gate is identical (TRAIN +$48 / +$44 differ only through the one-position
coupling; TEST +$1.81 both).

## 3. Placebo / null controls (2h continuation eligibility, tick, 1000 draws each)

Coverage problem found and handled: plain shifted flip timelines admit only
37% of continuation signals versus 61% for the real timeline, because real
flips are causally aligned with the continuation signals that follow them.
So three nulls were run: plain circular shifts (same events, less coverage),
coverage-matched circular shifts (same events, window widened per draw
until 61% — median 281 min), and random eligibility masks (61% of
continuation signals admitted at random, no flip information at all).

Fixed shifts (±6 h, ±12 h, +24 h): TRAIN −$41 … +$86, TEST +$59 … +$82,
all below the real gate, all at 40–44% coverage.

Reading: in the discovery half the real gate beats every null at the
97th–99th percentile. In the blind half it sits at the 84th–94th
percentile: better than most placebos but inside the range a coverage-
matched or random gate reaches one time in six. The full-sample
percentiles (98–99%) are carried by the discovery half.

| stop distance (pts) | 157 / 257 | 193 / 327 |
| stop / ATR14 | 3.9 / 6.1 | 4.0 / 5.6 |
| distance from flip price | 193 / 520 | 247 / 720 |
| 15-min range | 192 / 235 | 249 / 319 |
| efficiency since flip | 0.20 / 0.10 | 0.19 / 0.13 |
| 60-bar efficiency | 0.21 / 0.22 | 0.20 / 0.24 |
| CHoCH+repairs last 2h | 2 / 1 | 2 / 1 |

Not-eligible continuations are old trends (3–4 h+), far from the flip
price, with the pending dot far away: their stops are 1.6–1.7× wider and
their losses correspondingly larger (stale × wide-stop: TRAIN −$49 on 27
with avg loss −$11.8; TEST −$29 on 22, avg loss −$11.5). Hour of day,
volatility and 60-bar efficiency barely differ. So the gate measures
**trend age**, and trend age matters through two channels: an older trend
has a more distant protective dot (bigger dollar risk per trade) and a
lower continuation hit rate. It is not a volatility or "clean progress"
proxy — which agrees with E005.

## Selection-bias discussion

The gate was found on 2026-09-08 with `bt_chop.py` after bucketing
chocs/flips/repairs over the last 2 h and trying four gate variants, on
the full R0 sample (which contains TRAIN). The 2 h window was the one
window tried. This experiment's TRAIN half is therefore not independent
of the discovery; only TEST is. And in TEST the specific 2 h rule adds
nothing over no gate while the broader statement (stale continuations
lose) still holds. That is the pattern of a real but overstated effect:
the direction survives, the magnitude and the exact threshold do not.

## Verdict: **B** — the gate works, but the evidence for the 2 h rule is fragile and threshold-sensitive

- **Is 2 h special?** No. It is the best window in the half it was chosen
  on and neutral in the blind half, where 4 h is best. 30–60 min windows
  are harmful in both halves.
- **Is there a broad useful window?** Yes: continuations within 0–2 h of a
  flip are positive in both halves, ≥ 4 h are negative in both halves,
  2–4 h is undecided. The robust content is "do not continue a trend that
  has had no flip for 4 h+", not "2 h".
- **What does the gate measure?** Trend age. Old trends have distant dots
  (wider stops, larger losses) and lower continuation hit rates. Not
  volatility, not chop, not time of day.
- **How much of Owl's edge depends on it?** In TRAIN 88% (+$90 of +$102);
  in TEST 0% (+$135 either way); over R0 43% (+$101 of +$237). Half the
  historical edge and all of the discovery-half edge.
- **Does it beat realistic placebos?** In the discovery half, at the
  97th–99th percentile of three different nulls. In the blind half, at the
  84th–94th percentile: not on its own significant. Coverage-matched
  placebos need windows of ~4.7 h to reach the same coverage, which itself
  says the real events are unusually well aligned with the signals.
- **Reason to distrust the original discovery?** Yes, partly: the window
  was chosen on data that includes TRAIN, and the gain did not replicate in
  the blind half; the stale-trend penalty did. The gate should be kept (it
  removes a genuinely negative population in both halves), but the 2 h
  number should not be treated as tuned truth, and its contribution should
  be expected to be smaller live than the in-sample +$100 suggests.

Nothing changes in production from this experiment. Suggested follow-up
for ChatGPT's judgement, not executed: since the effect is really about
trend age and dot distance, the next question may be whether the loss on
stale continuations is explained by the stop width alone (E005 found the
narrowest stops bad, this finds the widest stale stops bad — both are
consistent with "risk per trade should be tied to structure age").
