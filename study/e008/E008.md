# E008 — Stop distance / risk geometry (2026-09-12)

Proposed by ChatGPT, executed by Claude Code. Production untouched.
Discovery: R0-TRAIN (ungated population, so stale continuations are in
it). Blind: R0-TEST. V1 historical only. Baseline = live config on ticks,
flat 0.02. Script `e008.py` → `results_e008.txt`; per-trade table
`trades_e008_ungated.csv` (570 trades: 237 flips, 333 continuations).
Constant-risk P&L = P&L in R × $4 (every trade risks $4 before costs).
Choices: 5 quintiles, 6 raw bands, 5 ratio tertiles, 5 cost bands, one
predeclared band rule, one secondary OLS, 1000 random-removal controls.

## 1. Stop-distance shape (continuations) — noise

| TRAIN quintile (pts) | n | wr | net | exp R | TEST | n | wr | net | exp R |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 ≤ 113 | 36 | 58% | −$0.4 | +0.04 | | 22 | 64% | +$6.9 | +0.14 |
| Q2 113–160 | 36 | 56% | −$2.3 | −0.01 | | 24 | 50% | −$6.8 | −0.11 |
| Q3 160–235 | 35 | 54% | −$5.4 | −0.03 | | 29 | 59% | +$10.1 | +0.05 |
| Q4 235–386 | 36 | 47% | **−$34.7** | −0.15 | | 38 | 68% | **+$49.7** | +0.23 |
| Q5 > 386 | 36 | 58% | +$25.9 | +0.05 | | 41 | 61% | +$7.5 | +0.10 |

Not monotone, not U-shaped, not inverted-U. The worst TRAIN quintile is
the best TEST quintile. Raw bands and all five ratio measures (stop/ATR,
stop/median range, stop/15-30-60 min range) change sign between halves.
For continuations, stop distance by itself carries no stable information.

## 2. Does stop width explain trend age? — no

Cross-tab (n / net / exp R), continuations, TRAIN width tertiles
(narrow ≤ 146, medium 146–272, wide > 272 pts):

| | narrow | medium | wide |
|---|---|---|---|
| TRAIN age ≤ 2h | 46 / +$12 / +0.13 | 32 / −$21 / −0.16 | 23 / **+$66** / +0.25 |
| TRAIN age ≥ 4h | 2 / 0 / −0.10 | 13 / +$1 / −0.04 | 23 / **−$57** / −0.14 |
| TEST age ≤ 2h | 30 / −$4 / −0.05 | 30 / +$20 / +0.14 | 32 / **+$62** / +0.29 |
| TEST age ≥ 4h | 4 / −$4 / −0.56 | 3 / −$13 / −1.01 | 20 / **−$20** / −0.01 |

Within the SAME width (wide), young continuations are strongly positive
in both halves and stale ones negative in both halves. Width does not
carry the effect; age does. Secondary OLS of P&L(R) on log(stop) + age
dummies: log(stop) coefficient ≈ 0 in both halves (t −0.01, +0.78); age
≥ 4h −0.17 R (t −0.9) and −0.38 R (t −1.9), essentially unchanged when
stop distance is dropped from the model. The age effect is weak in
t-stat terms but it is not a stop-width effect in disguise.

## 3. Risk normalisation — the penalty survives, the dollars shrink

| continuations by age | TRAIN fixed 0.02 / sum R / constant-risk | TEST fixed 0.02 / sum R / constant-risk |
|---|---|---|
| age ≤ 2h | +$56 / +6.5 R / +$26 | +$77 / +12.0 R / +$48 |
| age 2–4h | −$18 / −6.0 R / −$24 | +$27 / +8.1 R / +$32 |
| age ≥ 4h | **−$55 / −3.9 R / −$16** | **−$37 / −5.5 R / −$22** |

Stale continuations are negative in R in both halves (−0.10 R and
−0.20 R per trade). Fixed 0.02 sizing roughly triples their dollar
damage because their stops are wide (−$55 vs −$16, −$37 vs −$22). So the
answer to "signal or sizing" is BOTH: the signal is mildly negative and
the sizing amplifies it about 2–3×.

Awake gate under each normalisation (in-engine, all entries):

| | TRAIN gated / ungated | TEST gated / ungated |
|---|---|---|
| fixed 0.02 | +$102 / +$12 | +$135 / +$135 |
| sum R | +11.0 / −1.2 | +14.3 / **+18.5** |
| constant-risk $4 | +$44 / −$5 | +$57 / **+$74** |

Under constant risk the gate still adds ~$49 in the discovery half but
REMOVES $17 (4.2 R) in the blind half. Part of the gate's fixed-lot
benefit is that it happens to remove wide-stop trades, which the sizing
would otherwise have made expensive; sizing to constant risk does that
job on its own.

## 4. Cost fraction — arithmetic is real, the continuation data are too thin

Break-even win rate at RR 0.8 = (1 + c)/(1.8) with c = total cost / stop
(spread $7 + observed slippage ≈ 12 pt). Stops under 70 pt need 71%,
70–100 pt need 63%, 100–175 pt 61%, over 350 pt 57%. In the data the
< 100 pt continuation bands hold 8–15 trades per half and flip sign
(TRAIN 70–100: −$8 at 40% wr; TEST 70–100: +$8 at 88% wr). No mechanism
demonstrated for continuations. (See flips below.)

## 7. Flips — the one consistent geometry effect

| TRAIN flip quintile | n | wr | net | exp R | TEST | n | wr | net | exp R |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 ≤ 83 pt | 25 | 44% | **−$6.3** | −0.22 | | 25 | 40% | **−$9.6** | −0.30 |
| Q2 83–117 | 24 | 54% | 0.0 | −0.04 | | 16 | 56% | 0.0 | +0.01 |
| Q3 117–169 | 24 | 67% | +$10.3 | +0.20 | | 20 | 75% | +$19.1 | +0.35 |
| Q4 169–265 | 24 | 67% | +$19.3 | +0.20 | | 21 | 57% | +$3.7 | +0.03 |
| Q5 > 265 | 24 | 54% | +$5.8 | −0.03 | | 34 | 62% | +$54.5 | +0.11 |

The narrowest flip quintile (≤ 83 pt, cost ≥ 8% of the stop) loses in
both halves, in dollars and in R, with a 40–44% win rate against a 63%
break-even. This is the population behind the E005 "stop ≤ 98" survivor
(that rule was applied to the gated baseline, which is flip-heavy). It is
consistent with the cost mechanism, small (≈ −$8 per half), and already
covered by the `narrow_stop` forward-observation flag.

## 5. Candidate band — none (predeclared condition not met)

Rule was: both tails worse than the middle 60% in TRAIN. TRAIN quintile
expectancies −0.01, −0.06, −0.16, −0.96, **+0.72**: the wide tail is the
best quintile, so no candidate. The descriptive test of [q20, q80] on the
gated baseline confirms it would be harmful: blocking the wide side
removes +$60 (TRAIN) and +$34 (TEST) of winners (random-removal control:
the wide blocked set beats 100% / 91% of random subsets, i.e. it is the
best part), the narrow side removes ≈ $0. Rejected; not rescued.

## 6. Interaction with the awake gate (in-engine, tick)

| 2 awake 2h (live) | +$102 | +$135 | −$25.6 |

The band reproduces none of the gate's TRAIN benefit and damages TEST.
Stop geometry does not explain what awake does. (Config 3 is reported
because E007 asked for it; it is the best on TEST and on V1, but it is
descriptive, not a proposal, and its TRAIN result is below the live gate.)

## Verdict: **C** — stop geometry does not explain the awake / stale effect, and no rule survives

1. Stop distance vs expectancy for continuations: not a real relationship
   (sign flips between halves in every measure).
2. Narrow stops and costs: the arithmetic is real (a < 100 pt stop needs
   63–71% wins). It shows consistently only in the narrowest FLIP
   quintile (≤ 83 pt: −0.22 R / −0.30 R), not in continuations. Small.
3. Wide stale stops: the signal is worse (−0.10 R / −0.20 R in both
   halves) AND fixed-lot sizing multiplies the dollar loss by 2–3×.
4. After constant-risk normalisation the stale penalty remains, smaller.
5. Stop width explains none of the awake gate's benefit (OLS log(stop)
   ≈ 0; band-only and awake+band both worse than awake). What awake removes
   is age, and under constant risk the gate is TRAIN-only again (it costs
   4.2 R in TEST).
6. No stop-distance band survives; the predeclared condition failed in
   TRAIN and the descriptive band would have cut the best trades.
7. The continuation geometry pattern is noise. The one piece with a
   mechanism and two-half consistency is "narrowest flips lose", already
   under forward observation.

Not proposed, for ChatGPT's judgement: the risk-normalisation result
(Part 3) suggests the more interesting open question is sizing, not
entry — under constant risk per trade, stale-trend damage falls by
two thirds and the awake gate's blind-half contribution turns negative.

Production unchanged.
