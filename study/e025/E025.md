# E025 — pullback entry AT the trend's pending dot, stop at the next dot beyond (2026-09-15)

Owner's idea, preregistered before results (`PREREG_E025.md`, 15b6e89):
enter at the glowing dot of the trend instead of at the BOS close, with
the stop at the next lower low (next higher high for sells). Script
`e025.py`. Production unchanged.

## Two implementation bugs found and fixed before publishing anything
1. **Stop on the wrong side.** The first version used "the previous dot
   in time", which is not always below the entry dot (the dot sequence is
   not monotonic after a CHoCH or a bootstrap). That produced inverted
   trades that exited instantly. Fixed to the owner's wording: the most
   recent dot that actually sits *below* the entry (above, for sells).
2. **Broken random-direction control.** When the control flipped the
   direction it kept the original stop price, which then sat on the
   profit side and booked a free +1R: the control showed PF 2.5–3.4.
   Fixed by mirroring the geometry (`sl = e − d·dist`) for flipped trades
   only; the primary arm keeps the exact dot price as its stop.
Geometry verified after the fix: winners +0.800R, losers −0.990R (the
0.01R slip is gap-through on ticks), 8.6% of stops outside a tight band.

## How often the level is reached
Only **24–30% of armed dots are ever touched**: 393 trades from 1319
armed levels on R0, and the same ratio in every era. Seven trends out of
ten leave without a pullback to the protected dot. That is the structural
cost of a limit entry.

On R0 the direction call matters: +$44 against −$300 for the same
entries taken by coin flip. That is the cleanest control separation seen
in this whole series. But the arm is negative in TRAIN and only positive
in TEST, and it earns a fifth of the baseline.

The arithmetic explains it: at a 0.8R target a strategy needs **56%**
wins to break even. This one gets **47%**. Entering at the protected dot
means buying just before the structure breaks often enough to eat the
edge.

## Pass criteria
Beats BASELINE in TRAIN: **no** (−9.18 vs +102.22). In TEST: yes.
≥ 3 of 5 eras on expectancy: yes (3/5). Beats its own random-direction
control: **on R0 yes, across the eras no.**

## Verdict: **C — not adopted, but the least dead of the owner's ideas so far.**

Two of the four checks pass. What kills it is the win rate against a
0.8R target and the fact that the direction advantage seen on R0 vanishes
on 2022–2026. If this family is revisited, the registered question should
be the target, not the entry: the same entries with a target at 1.5–2R
would only need 33–40% wins. That is a different experiment and must be
registered before it is run, on data not used here.

Production unchanged.
