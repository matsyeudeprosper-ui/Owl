So: a trailing drawdown of $60 is ordinary (one run in four over a

## 3–5. Kill rules on the real sequences (blind on TEST)

Absolute-net lines (−48, −60, −63, −95) never trigger in TRAIN, TEST or
V1. Trailing-DD lines: 73 (the observed TRAIN max) would have fired in
TRAIN at trade 72 with +$103 still to come, and in TEST at trade 172 with
+$107 to come; 79 fires in TEST at trade 187 forgoing +$119; 92 and 120
never fire. Every rolling-N rule ($ and R, p95 and p99) never fires
anywhere: the historical worst windows sit just above the thresholds
(worst rolling-20 −54 vs −60 line). False-stop probability per 265
trades by construction: p95 rules 5%, p99 rules 1%, live −60 5.8%.

R-based vs dollar-based: the R statistics are the stable ones (section
1), so an R-based line would transfer between periods better; but on
this sample neither kind triggered, so there is no blind evidence either
way about detection.

## 6. After a deep drawdown, what happens next (unchanged strategy)

| first crossing of | reached in | next 20 trades median | next 40 trades median | P(back at high within 40) | P(deepens 20+ within 40) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DD ≥ 40 | 66% of paths | +$8 | +$17 | 20% | 29% |
| DD ≥ 60 | 27% | +$8 | +$16 | 4% | 29% |
| DD ≥ 80 | 10% | +$9 | +$18 | 1% | 29% |

Historical: TRAIN's one $60 crossing was followed by +$40 over 40
trades; TEST's five crossings by a median +$18 over 40 trades, but all
five deepened by another $20 first. With an intact edge, stopping at a
deep drawdown locks in the bottom: the expected next 40 trades are
+$16 regardless of depth. The drawdown depth by itself says nothing
about whether the edge is intact — which is exactly what Part 8 tests.

## 7. Composite and the E005 breaker

Composite (depth AND concentration): never fires on any real sequence,
0.9% false positives. The E005 rolling-20 state machine reaches PAUSED
in TRAIN (trade 60, forgoing +$79), in TEST (trade 27, forgoing +$155)
and in V1, and in 85% of unchanged bootstrap paths: as a kill it is
useless; it is a pause signal that fires on ordinary variance.

## 8. Detecting real edge decay (3 000 degraded bootstrap paths of 265 trades per scenario)

Detection rate (median trade of trigger):

| TRAIL 120 (p99) | 1.2% | 17% | 71% | 21% | 5% | 35% | 51% |
| roll-20 $ −60 (p95) | 4.7% | 14% | 36% | 9% | 7% | 24% | 25% |
| roll-30 $ −66 (p95) | 4.9% | 18% | 47% | 14% | 9% | 29% | 34% |
| roll-10 $ −57 (p99) | 1.2% | 2% | 7% | 2% | 2% | 6% | 3% |
| composite | 1.0% | 7% | 28% | 5% | 3% | 16% | 18% |
| E005 → PAUSED | 87% | 98% | 100% | 98% | 93% | 100% | 100% |

Reading:
- Edge decay in this strategy is a slow bleed, not a burst: even a 20%
  winner-to-loser conversion (expectancy −0.48/trade, a broken strategy)
  needs about 90 trades to reach −$60. Rolling-window rules, built to
  catch concentrated losses, miss slow bleeds almost entirely (14–36% at
  the same 5% false-positive cost).
- At a matched ~5% false-positive rate the absolute-net line is the best
  detector of every scenario, and the live −$60 is within 2 points of the
  frozen p95 line (−$63). Trailing-DD lines are slower (they wait for a
  peak first). The composite and the p99 lines buy fewer false stops at
  the price of missing most real decay.
- Nothing detects a mild decay (S1, expectancy ≈ 0) reliably: 41% within
  265 trades at best. A strategy that has merely stopped earning is
  statistically invisible for months.

|---|---|---|---|
| TRAIN | +$102 / 73 | +$83 / 84 / 16 / 30 / 0.05 | not fired |

The layer deepens drawdowns slightly (+$5 to +$11), keeps the account
under water 89–94% of the time (same as flat: the book is nearly always
in some drawdown), and raises the false-kill probability of the −$60 line
from 5.8% to **9.2%** per 265 trades (bootstrap of the full layer; its
maxDD p50 54, p95 100, p99 127). Fighters and adds make the line more
likely to fire on variance without changing what it can detect.

## 10. Account scale (fixed 0.02, unchanged strategy)

- Normal drawdown over a month: about $46; one month in ten $79; one in
  twenty $92; one in a hundred $120. Over a 100-trade review window:
  $31 / $57 / $67 / $88.
- Time under water: typically 78 trades (~10 days); one month in twenty
  has 200+ trades (~4 weeks) under the previous high.
- Where −$60 sits: about the 95th percentile of net-from-start over a
  month (5.8% false kill; 9.2% with the production layer); ~3% over the
  100-trade review window.
- A statistically justified stop looks like what is already there: an
  absolute line near the p95 of startup ruin (−$60 to −$65), accepted as
  a 1-in-17 false stop per month, with the knowledge that it detects a
  broken strategy (S2) nine times in ten and a merely flat one (S1) four
  times in ten. Not deployed; nothing to deploy.

## Verdict: **B** — some approaches help, none reliably distinguish variance from failure; monitoring only

1. Is −$60 inside normal variance? As a trailing drawdown, yes (26% of
   months). As the live rule (net from start), no: ~6% of months.
2. False-kill probability of −$60: 5.8% per 265 trades flat, 9.2% with
   the production layer, ~3% over a 100-trade window.
3. Does a fixed-dollar line make sense? For this strategy's failure mode
   (slow bleed) it is the best of the simple detectors at equal false-
   positive cost. Its weakness is dollar instability: one wide-stop loss
   is a third of a drawdown, which R-based statistics do not suffer from.
4. Rolling P&L or rolling R: rolling windows of either kind detect
   concentrated loss, which is not how this strategy fails. R is the more
   stable statistic between halves; neither triggered blind.
5. The E005 rolling-20 breaker: 85–87% false PAUSED rate; not a failure
   signal. Keep it as the informational state it is.
6. Best separator of luck from decay: absolute net at ~p95, i.e. the
   existing line. Trailing DD is slower; windows and composites miss slow
   decay.
7. Blind survival: every frozen rule "survived" TEST by never firing; the
   only rules that fired (trailing 73/79) fired falsely and forgo $107–119.
8. Speed on simulated decay: ~90 trades for a broken strategy, ~116 for a
   mildly negative one when it fires at all (41%).
9. Forward shadow candidate: none new. The observer already records the
   rolling-20 state; the informative quantity to add is the cumulative
   R since the era start alongside dollars, so that a wide-stop outlier
   does not masquerade as a regime. Added as columns (informational).
