## Answers
1. **Price-down + OI-down predicts a rebound?** Not at 1–15 min after costs (negative at all three, 0th/93rd/38th percentile). At 30–60 min the events drift up, but so do price-only shocks and even upside shocks.
2. **Stronger than price-down alone?** No. Control A is better at 15 m (+15.7 vs −5.0) and only slightly worse at 60 m (+25.5 vs +58.3, both dominated by a few events).
3. **Stronger than OI-down alone?** OI-down alone is flat to negative (−1 to −15 at 1–30 m, +6 at 60 m); adding the price shock does not turn it into a rebound.
4. **Survives Exness costs?** No at the primary horizon.
5. **Survives both halves?** No: discovery +24, check −41 at 15 m; robustness variant −59 in the check half.
6. **Independent of extreme events?** No: 15 m goes from −5 to −42 without the top 10%; 60 m from +58 to −18.
7. **Liquidation / taker context strengthens it?** No. Events are not sell-heavy in taker flow, carry almost no liquidation, and the few with real liquidation cascades are the worst performers.
8. **How often?** 4/day (151 in 38 scored days), 15% of days empty, median spacing 2.9 h.
9. **Strong and slow enough to trade?** No. The only positive horizon (60 m) is a heavy-tailed drift shared by the controls, with a $48 drawdown per $1.17 expected at 0.02 lot.
10. **One raw mechanism worth carrying forward?** Not from E018. What remains is E015's forced-liquidation snap-back, under forward test in E017 (blind, no verdict before 30 events).

## Verdict: **C — OI contraction adds no tradable information beyond the price move.**

The defining comparison fails in the wrong direction: price-down without
OI-down does better than price-down with OI-down at the frozen horizon,
and the largest deleveraging events are the worst. Nothing retuned; the
60-minute cell is recorded as descriptive only.

Production unchanged. E017 frozen and blind.
